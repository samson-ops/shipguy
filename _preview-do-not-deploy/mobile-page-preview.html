How Car Shipping in Mobile Works, Step by Step

The whole process has five simple steps. Most customers finish step one in about a minute.

Step 1: Get your quote. Use the form at the top of this page, or call our Mobile office. Tell us the pickup city, the delivery city, and your dates. Then we answer with a real price, not a teaser number.

Step 2: Book your spot. Once you approve the quote, we post your car to our carrier network. Remember, you pay nothing at this step. Your card is not charged until a carrier is assigned.

Step 3: Meet the driver. The driver calls you a day ahead to set a time. Together, you walk around the car and note its condition on the Bill of Lading. Take photos too. Then the driver loads the car and heads out.

Step 4: Track the trip. While your car rides, we stay in touch. Plus, you can call or text your dispatcher any day from 6 AM to 11 PM. If anything changes, you hear it from us first.

Step 5: Take delivery. At drop-off, you inspect the car before you sign. Compare it to your pickup photos. If everything looks right, you sign, and the job is done. That is the whole process from start to finish.

Why Car Shipping in Mobile Is Fast

Mobile sits at a busy crossroads. I-10 runs east and west along the whole Gulf Coast. I-65 starts here and runs north through Alabama. Because of this, car hauling trucks pass through Mobile every day of the week.

Why does that matter to you? The answer is simple. More trucks means more choices for your shipment. Better yet, more choices means faster pickup and a better price.

Think about the main routes. New Orleans is about two hours west, so that lane runs daily. Florida traffic rolls east on I-10 all year long. Birmingham, Nashville, and Chicago all connect through I-65. In short, your car can leave Mobile in almost any direction without a long wait.

The junction also helps with tricky routes. For example, say you need to ship a car to a small town in Kansas. No truck goes there straight from Mobile. However, plenty of trucks go to Dallas or Memphis. So we build the trip in steps and still keep the price fair. The roads do half the work for us.

Shipping a Car Through the Port of Mobile

The Port of Mobile is one of the busiest ports in the country. In fact, ships here carry vehicles across the ocean. The industry calls this roll-on/roll-off, or RO/RO for short. Cars drive onto the ship at one port. Then they drive off at the next one.

Here is our part, in plain terms. We handle the land side of the trip. If you are exporting, we pick up your car anywhere in the U.S. Then we deliver it to your freight forwarder near the port. Ports only accept cars during set hours, so timing matters. We schedule the drop-off to match their window. Your forwarder books the ship, handles the title papers, and clears customs.

Importing works the same way, just in reverse. Once customs releases your car, we pick it up at the port. After that, we deliver it to any address in the country. One phone call covers the whole land trip.

We handle these work moves every week. We ship cars for new hires before their first day. We ship them home again when a contract wraps up. Hurricane Season: What Really Happens

Hurricane season runs from June through November. Most of those weeks are calm. Still, you deserve to know exactly how we handle a storm.

We watch the forecast all season long. If a storm threatens the Gulf, we call you early. Then you have simple choices. You can move your pickup earlier. You can also push it back until the weather clears. Either way, we work out the dates with you.

Some people ship a car OUT before a storm arrives. That is smart planning. However, trucks fill up fast once a storm has a name. So if you want your car moved ahead of a storm, call right away. Waiting even one day can cost you a spot.

Mardi Gras and Downtown Pickups

Mobile is the birthplace of Mardi Gras in America. The parades are wonderful. They also close many downtown streets for weeks at a time.

A 75-foot car hauler cannot park on a parade route. So during the season, we plan around it. We meet you a few blocks away, outside the barricades. The handoff takes the same few minutes as always. Best of all, your delivery date does not change.

Snowbird Season at the Alabama Beaches

Every winter, the Baldwin County beaches fill up. Visitors from the Midwest and Canada spend the cold months in Gulf Shores and Orange Beach. Locals call them snowbirds.

Snowbirds ship a lot of cars. Most arrive between October and December. Then they head home in March and April. Those months get busy, so book 2 to 3 weeks ahead.

Here is a money-saving tip. Book your round trip as one order. We plan both legs at once, and you skip a second round of calls. We also serve Fairhope and Point Clear all year for the folks who came for a season and stayed for good. Planning a winter stay? Start with our snowbird shipping page.

What Is Inside Your Mobile Quote

Every honest quote has two parts. The first part pays the truck driver. The second part pays us for planning the trip. The driver’s share is the bigger one, and it moves with the market.

So what moves the price? Fuel costs, the season, and the route. Busy routes cost less, because many trucks want the load. Quiet routes cost more, because fewer trucks pass that way. A Friday-only pickup on a narrow street also adds cost. Flexible dates almost always save you money.

One warning before you shop around. If a quote is hundreds of dollars below everyone else, be careful. That low number is often bait. The company posts your car at a cheap rate, and no driver takes it. Then your dates slip, or the price jumps at the last minute. We quote the real number the first time. It is better business, and it is more honest.

How to Get Your Car Ready for Pickup

Ten minutes of prep makes pickup day smooth. Follow this short list.

First, wash the car. A clean car makes the inspection easy. Small chips and scratches show clearly, so the Bill of Lading is accurate. That protects you.

Next, take photos. Walk around the car and shoot every side. Include the roof and the odometer. Date-stamped phone photos are perfect. Then keep them until the car arrives.

Then, empty the car. Take out anything valuable. You may leave up to about 100 pounds in the trunk, but the carrier’s insurance does not cover personal items. Also remove toll tags and parking passes, because they can rack up charges on the truck.

Also, check the basics. Leave about a quarter tank of gas. After all, a full tank only adds weight. Likewise, fix or report any leaks before pickup. Fold in the mirrors and remove loose accessories, like bike racks.

Finally, plan the handoff. Have your key ready, plus a spare if you can. Pick a meeting spot with room for a big truck. After that, you are set. From there, the driver handles the rest.
